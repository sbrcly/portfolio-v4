Verify

01

The problem

Faith apps borrow the wrong mechanics.

Most of them are habit trackers in vestments: streaks, scores, completion rings, and the quiet guilt of a missed day. Those mechanics reward showing up and punish honesty.

What I wanted already existed and was two thousand years old: the Church's week, its lectionary, its prayers, its creeds. Prava puts that at the center across twelve traditions.

The founding rule is record, not score. It turned out to be an engineering constraint that shaped the schema, the prompts, and what the app refuses to measure.

02

What was built

A full consumer product, run by one person.

A native-feeling iOS app with a daily journal, prayer and scripture surfaces, a weekly lectionary, and small accountability circles. The AI teaches and reflects. It never touches the Church's fixed texts.

Platform
Next.js and TypeScript inside Capacitor as a native iOS app
AI
Thirteen grounded surfaces, versioned prompts, snapshot-tested fallbacks
Data
Postgres with Prisma, additive-only migrations
Revenue
Freemium subscriptions, monthly and annual
03

Three decisions

Decisions I would defend in any interview.

i

AI cost in three waves

Waste first. Then small-model routing where quality held. Then prompt caching, with a usage-event table that checks the provider's cache metrics. A cost optimization you cannot measure is a rumor.

ii

Philosophy as a schema constraint

The database stores what happened, never a grade. A missed day is recorded honestly and never becomes a punishment mechanic.

iii

The process is the second engineer

Additive-only migrations, snapshot-tested prompt fallbacks, features dark-shipped behind flags with written flip runbooks.

04

The back office

Eleven internal tools nobody sees.

The Prompt Lab versions every system prompt and shows history, diffs, and drift. The Profile Simulator runs the selection algorithm against a built profile, scores included.

05

Outcome

Live, used across a dozen denominations, paying for itself.

On the App Store since Easter 2026, with paying subscribers on monthly and annual plans. Specific numbers are available in an interview.
